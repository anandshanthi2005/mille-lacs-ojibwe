Discover the history, sovereignty, culture, and contemporary life of a living Anishinaabe nation in Minnesota.

Who are the Mille Lacs Band?

The Mille Lacs Band of Ojibwe is a federally recognized, sovereign tribal nation in Minnesota. The Band is part of the wider Anishinaabe (Ojibwe) people. Its homeland centers on the Mille Lacs Lake region, where generations have maintained relationships with the land, waters, and community.

Remember: Indigenous nations are not only part of history. The Mille Lacs Band is a living government and community today.

01 · History & Ancestral Lands

Rooted in place

  • Ojibwe people belong to the wider Anishinaabe peoples of the Great Lakes region.
  • Mille Lacs Lake and the surrounding lands are important to the Band’s history and identity.
  • U.S. treaties and policies affected land ownership and access to resources.
  • In the 1837 treaty, Ojibwe leaders ceded land while reserving hunting, fishing, and gathering rights.
  • The Band continues to protect its homeland and treaty rights.
02 · Government & Sovereignty

A nation that governs

  • A Band is a distinct Indigenous political community, not a musical group.
  • Tribal sovereignty means the inherent right of a Native nation to govern itself.
  • The Mille Lacs Band government has Executive, Legislative, and Judicial branches.
  • The Chief Executive leads the Executive Branch; the Band Assembly makes laws; the Tribal Court interprets and applies laws.
  • The government provides services and supports its citizens.
03 · Culture & Traditions

Living traditions

  • Ojibwe language, family, storytelling, and community relationships carry knowledge across generations.
  • Wild rice, called manoomin, is an important traditional food and cultural resource.
  • Fishing, maple sugar making, and harvesting reflect deep knowledge of local ecosystems.
  • Art, music, dance, and gatherings express culture and identity.
  • Practices and experiences vary among Ojibwe families and individuals.
04 · Life Today

Community & future

  • Band citizens study, work, raise families, and lead in Minnesota and beyond.
  • Programs support education, health, community development, and natural resources.
  • Protecting treaty rights, the environment, and cultural knowledge remains important.
  • Language revitalization and youth learning help connect generations.
  • Band citizens shape their own priorities and future.

One important takeaway

The Mille Lacs Band of Ojibwe is a sovereign, living Anishinaabe nation. Its people continue to exercise self-government, sustain culture, care for their homeland, and shape the future.
