Mille Lacs Band of Ojibwe

A living Anishinaabe nation: explore its homeland, self-government, culture, community and future.

Welcome · Boozhoo

The Non-Removable Mille Lacs Band of Ojibwe is a sovereign, federally recognized tribal nation in east-central Minnesota. Its citizens maintain cultural knowledge and govern their communities today. This learning site begins with the Band's own published history and explanations.

Sovereignty and government today

Tribal sovereignty means the inherent right of a tribal nation to govern itself. The Mille Lacs Band uses a separation-of-powers system created through Band governance reforms in the 1980s.

Executive branch

Led by the Chief Executive, it implements laws and provides services through departments including Education, Natural Resources, Health and Human Services, and Community Development.

Legislative branch

The Band Assembly consists of three District Representatives and a Speaker of the Assembly / Secretary-Treasurer. It passes laws, allocates funds and oversees legislation.

Judicial branch

The Court of Central Jurisdiction includes a trial court and a Court of Appeals, helping apply Band laws and protect rights under those laws.

Life today: learning, services and stewardship

The Band operates Nay Ah Shing Schools, supports Ojibwe language and cultural education, provides health services, and manages natural resources. Its communities also create employment and business opportunities. Cultural practices such as wild rice harvesting, fishing, sugarbushing, beadwork and community gatherings continue alongside contemporary life.

Ojibwe language and the Band's seven values

Boozhoo is a greeting used in Anishinaabemowin. Manoomin means wild rice. The seven values below use the spellings and English descriptions published on the Mille Lacs Band's culture page. Select a value to read its meaning. Spellings and pronunciations may vary across Ojibwe communities.

For authentic pronunciation and language teaching, consult the Band's culture resources and its Ojibwe Language and Culture Program. We have not invented audio pronunciations.
